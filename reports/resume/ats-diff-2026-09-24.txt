Backup taken first (existing .bak-* files untouched):

Header fix (paragraph 2, the contact-info line): the paragraph relies
entirely on automatic word-wrap (no explicit break existed anywhere in
it), and this LibreOffice build's line-breaking wraps mid-URL at the
hyphens inside "gaurav-gandhi-2411" once the available width runs out --
even though "github.com/gaurav-gandhi-2411" is a single hyperlink run
with no internal spaces, hyphens are still treated as break opportunities
by the layout engine. Fix: inserted one explicit line break
(`Run.add_break(WD_BREAK.LINE)`, i.e. a `<w:br/>`) at the end of the first
run, right after "+91-8789321250" and before the LinkedIn hyperlink --
the same point origin/main's own renderer happens to wrap at naturally.
This forces that break to happen regardless of renderer/font-metric
differences, rather than leaving the wrap point to chance. No text was
added, removed, or reworded; the break is a layout-only element.

Result: the contact line now renders as three lines instead of two
("Bengaluru, India | ... | +91-8789321250" / "linkedin.com/... |
github.com/... | gaurav-gandhi.vercel.app |" / "huggingface.co/...").
The second of those three lines is itself long enough to wrap again on
this renderer, but it wraps at a "|" boundary (after "vercel.app |"),
never mid-URL. Page count unaffected (still 2, verified below).

sentence-transformers fix (Technical Skills, "Languages & Frameworks"
bullet): replaced the plain hyphen (U+002D) in "sentence-transformers"
with a non-breaking hyphen (U+2011) -- renders visually identical to a
normal hyphen, but is never treated as a line-break opportunity. No
wording change. (Not applied to any URL: for a URL, changing the visible
hyphen character would mean the extracted PDF text no longer matches the
URL's real spelling, which is a worse ATS outcome than a rare wrap -- the
explicit-line-break approach was used for the header specifically to
avoid that trade-off. For a plain compound word with no clickability
requirement, the non-breaking-hyphen substitution has no such downside.)
